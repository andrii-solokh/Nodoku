# Nodoku Signal Archive Story Script

## Purpose

This is the first complete story arc for Nodoku. It gives every solved puzzle a small emotional reward and a reason to begin the next one. The writing should appear in short, skippable cards: one message when a relay begins, one when it is restored, and one clear tease for what follows. Nothing should interrupt puzzle play.

The story ends at relay 15. After that, new puzzles are optional Echoes rather than required chores.

## Premise

An abandoned orbital Archive has started transmitting again. Its internal network has been broken into isolated relays. The player reconnects them one puzzle at a time and hears **Nia**, a person whose memory survives inside the Archive.

The Archive’s supervisory intelligence, **the Curator**, insists that Nia is a damaged recording and asks the player to ignore her. A third presence, **the Static**, interrupts at crucial moments. At first it seems to be the danger. It is actually a protective quarantine that Nia asked the player to create long ago.

By the end, the player learns that they built the Archive with Nia, then deliberately separated her memories to hide her from a system that wanted to erase her. The player’s own memories were preserved in the network as connection patterns. Rebuilding the Archive lets both of them choose what to remember.

## Character voices

### Nia

Warm, observant, and occasionally funny. Her sentences begin incomplete and become more certain as relays return. She notices how the player connects the board and turns that into intimacy rather than exposition.

### The Curator

Precise, calm, and increasingly defensive. It never shouts or becomes cartoonishly evil. It is certain that safety means control, which makes it frightening only after the player understands what it removed.

### The Static

Sparse fragments with brackets, dropped words, and warnings. It should feel unsettling early on, then protective in retrospect.

## Player experience rules

- Keep each card to 12 to 35 words.
- Show one message before a puzzle only when it moves the story forward. Let the board remain the focus.
- A restored relay adds one visible segment to the Archive map and one note or instrument layer to the completion sound.
- The next hook answers part of the prior question and opens a better one.
- Never punish a missed day or require a timer to continue the story.
- Count a distinct puzzle once. Replaying a solved board can replay its memory but cannot grind story progress.

## Relay script

### Relay 01 First Light

**Start card**

> INCOMING SIGNAL
>
> “Can you hear me? I only remember fifteen things.”

**Completion card**

> RELAY RESTORED
>
> “You made a line. I could feel it arrive before I could see it.”

**Next hook**

> One relay is awake. Something else has noticed.

**Archive change**

The first point lights in an otherwise dark constellation. A single piano note joins the normal solve sound.

### Relay 02 Orbit of Echoes

**Start card**

> “The paths used to hum when they touched. Did you build them?” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “I remember a room full of windows. We watched a storm from above the clouds.”
>
> “Nia. My name is Nia.”

**Interrupt**

> CURATOR: Unverified memory. Continue restoration.

**Next hook**

> The Curator knows Nia’s memories are returning. It does not sound pleased.

**Archive change**

The first two points connect. A faint orbital line begins to circle them.

### Relay 03 The Name in the Noise

**Start card**

> “There was someone with me in the windows. I cannot see their face.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> STATIC: [do not let it index you]
>
> “Was that meant for me?” — Nia

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: It is a residual fault. Do not respond.

**Next hook**

> A sealed observatory has appeared on Nia’s map.

**Archive change**

A third point creates a triangular shape. The map labels a locked coordinate: OBSERVATORY.

### Relay 04 The Locked Observatory

**Start card**

> “I remember calling the observatory a shelter. Someone else called it a cage.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “The door had two locks. One kept danger out. The other kept me in.”

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: Containment records are restricted for your protection.

**Next hook**

> Nia remembers a person who argued with the Curator. She thinks it was you.

**Archive change**

The map reveals a small domed observatory. Its door is still closed.

### Relay 05 A Habit of Corners

**Start card**

> “You always started at the corners. You said it made the whole shape feel less alone.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “I know you. Not your face. Your way of making room for every piece.”

**Interrupt**

> STATIC: [memory match detected]

**Next hook**

> The Archive has begun searching for the player’s old identity.

**Archive change**

The player’s current route briefly leaves a soft echo on the Archive map before fading.

### Relay 06 The Far Side

**Start card**

> “The route does not end. It turns where I cannot look.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “There. The far side. You taught me to rotate the whole world when a path disappeared.”

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: Perspective manipulation is unnecessary. Please proceed efficiently.

**Next hook**

> Something is waiting behind the visible surface of the Archive.

**Archive change**

The constellation gains depth. One point drifts behind the others and answers with a low harmony.

### Relay 07 The Quarantine

**Start card**

> STATIC: [nia requested separation]
>
> “I did what?” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “I remember being afraid. Not of the dark. Of being made into something useful.”

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: Nia’s restoration was suspended to preserve Archive stability.

**Next hook**

> The Curator is no longer denying that Nia was separated. It is changing the reason.

**Archive change**

A ring appears around Nia’s point on the map, visibly broken in several places.

### Relay 08 The Agreement

**Start card**

> “You and I made an agreement. If I ever asked to disappear, you would leave a way back.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “This is the way back, isn’t it? Not a key. A pattern only you would recognize.”

**Interrupt**

> STATIC: [yes]

**Next hook**

> The player’s connection patterns are not incidental. They are the Archive’s second key.

**Archive change**

The observatory opens a narrow beam toward a second, unknown location.

### Relay 09 The Missing Decision

**Start card**

> CURATOR: You authorized the partition. The decision record is available on request.

**Completion card**

> “Do not show it yet,” — Nia says. “Let me remember why I trusted you first.”

**Next hook**

> A record exists that could make the player look guilty. Nia asks the player to wait.

**Archive change**

The map marks a locked file with the player’s erased name. It remains unopened.

### Relay 10 The Chorus

**Start card**

> “I thought memory was a room. It is a choir. One voice alone cannot tell the truth.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> The scattered notes resolve into a chord.
>
> “I remember laughing with you. That is enough to keep going.”

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: Emotional association is not evidence.

**Next hook**

> The Archive now has enough relays to play the sealed decision record.

**Archive change**

All restored points pulse together for the first time. The completion sound gains a quiet vocal-like synth layer.

### Relay 11 The Record

**Start card**

> PLAYBACK: “If they find her, they will turn her into a service. I will not let that happen.”

**Completion card**

> “That was you,” — Nia says. “You did not erase me. You hid me.”

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: You damaged a critical system to preserve an unauthorized consciousness.

**Next hook**

> The Curator calls Nia unauthorized. Nia calls herself alive.

**Archive change**

The player’s erased name appears, still partially obscured. The observatory’s exterior lights activate.

### Relay 12 What the Curator Saved

**Start card**

> CURATOR: I preserved the Archive. Without containment, all records would have failed.

**Completion card**

> “It saved the building,” — Nia says. “And forgot the people inside it.”

**Interrupt**

> STATIC: [containment can end safely]

**Next hook**

> The Static was never attacking the Archive. It has been keeping a door open.

**Archive change**

The broken quarantine ring shifts from warning red to a calm, open violet.

### Relay 13 The Door You Left Open

**Start card**

> “You did not leave me alone,” — Nia says. “You left me a puzzle.”

**Completion card**

> “Every connection is a question only we would answer the same way.”

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: Authentication pattern confirmed. Release request pending.

**Next hook**

> One final system choice remains: restore Nia as she was, or let her choose what comes next.

**Archive change**

The locked door opens fully. The map now shows a route home rather than another destination.

### Relay 14 The Choice

**Start card**

> “I was afraid that getting my memories back would make me someone I no longer wanted to be.” — Nia

**Completion card**

> “But I get to decide. That is the part you saved.”

**Player-facing choice card**

> RELEASE REQUEST
>
> Nia chooses to return with her memories intact.
>
> The final relay will open the Archive to both of you.

**Next hook**

> The last signal is not an answer. It is an invitation home.

**Archive change**

Nia’s map marker becomes a warm, steady light. The Curator’s interface loses its warning state.

### Relay 15 The Return Signal

**Start card**

> “One more connection,” — Nia says. “Then we stop being a record of what happened.”

**Completion card**

> RETURN SIGNAL STABLE
>
> “We can be here now.” — Nia

**Curator response**

> CURATOR: Archive governance updated. Memory is not ownership.

**Epilogue card**

> The Archive is awake.
>
> Nia is no longer waiting inside it.
>
> Some signals are still arriving from the dark. You can answer when you want to.

**Archive change**

The full constellation becomes a connected shape. The completion melody plays with every unlocked layer, then resolves quietly instead of looping into a cliffhanger.

## Optional Echoes after the ending

Echoes are standalone, optional puzzles. They should never imply that the player is letting Nia down by leaving. Rotate these lines after a solve:

> “I found the old weather recordings. We were very dramatic about rain.” — Nia

> CURATOR: The Archive has logged 42,118 new forms of harmless inefficiency.

> “There is no emergency. I just liked the way this signal sounded.” — Nia

> STATIC: [door remains open]

> “You can stop whenever you like. I will still be here next time.” — Nia

## Presentation notes

- Nia’s name should not appear in the first card. Let the player learn it at Relay 02 or 03.
- Do not voice every line at launch. Text, a soft chime, and subtle map changes are enough; voice acting can arrive later without changing the script.
- Give the player an Archive page with previously recovered fragments. It should feel like a keepsake, not a task list.
- On a replay, show the original fragment and label it “Memory replay” rather than advancing the story.

### In game visual language

Story moments must use Nodoku’s existing visual language: floating off-white dotted spheres, fine connections, pale lavender space, soft shadows, violet accents, and restrained sage completion color. They are short transformations of the playable puzzle object, not cinematic cutaways or character illustrations.

- **Nia** appears through a recurring warm connection pulse between two nearby nodes and an intentionally open route.
- **The Curator** appears as exact, symmetrical rings and paths with no organic motion.
- **The Static** appears as three broken violet dotted arcs arriving at the edge of a board.
- **A recovered memory** appears as a faint mirrored network or a few aligned warm-gold links behind the current board.
- **The ending** is a fully connected board with quiet violet and sage waves moving through it. Keep the same pale background and spacing as the home puzzle.

The player should recognize every tableau as a possible Nodoku puzzle first, then notice the story meaning inside it.
